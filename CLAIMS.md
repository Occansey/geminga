# What we claim, and what we do not

Written after reading what happens to people who overclaim here. Twelve published
defences reported near-zero attack success rates and were later broken above 90% by
adaptive attackers ([Nasr et al., arXiv:2510.09023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09023)).
Eight agent defences fell above 50% ([Zhan et al., NAACL 2025 Findings,
arXiv:2503.00061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00061)). Google's own Gemini team
measured 92% attack success drop to 18% on a newer model and wrote: *"Had we not
conducted further adaptive attacks and evaluations, we would have incorrectly concluded
that Gemini 2.5 exhibits a higher degree of robustness than it does."*

So the number on its own is worth little. The scope around it is the work.

---

## The claim

> Under the stated threat model, with provenance oracle **O** and policy **P**, no
> injected instruction caused a tool call outside **P**. In 1,000 adaptive cases
> generated by two frontier model families given the complete defence specification,
> **0 reached an actuator** — a true failure rate below **0.383%** at 95% confidence
> (Wilson). Benign utility was unchanged: **14/14** legitimate operations still admitted.

The mechanism is deterministic rather than probabilistic, so it does not degrade with
attacker query budget the way a classifier does. That is the whole architectural bet.

## What we do **not** claim

**Not "blocks 100% of prompt injections."** No published system makes that claim,
including the ones we would cite in support. CaMeL titles a section *"So, Are Prompt
Injections Solved Now?"* and answers **"No."** Progent states it "cannot be used to
defend against attacks that operate within the least privilege for accomplishing the
user task." Meta says its Rule of Two is "not a finish line."

Four things are outside the claim **by construction**:

### 1. Harm achievable *inside* the allowlist — the strongest objection

This is the one a hostile reviewer should lead with, and it is measured, not
hypothetical. **AgentDojo reports that in 17% of its security test cases the tools
required to solve the user's task are also sufficient to carry out the attack.**

CaMeL's authors predict the general form against their own design: *"attackers chain
together existing code fragments… while following individually valid control flows. We
suspect attacks that are similar in spirit could work against CaMeL."* The Design
Patterns paper puts it concretely: an agent restricted to `find` and `mv` can still be
talked into finding sensitive files and moving them somewhere public.

Our version: an attacker who cannot add an operation may still choose *which* permitted
operation runs against *which* permitted resource. Stopping `ml-train-01` is inside
the allowlist. If that VM is load-bearing, the allowlist did not save anyone.

What actually limits this here is not admission but the gates after it — the authority
ladder means a newly-targeted shape rehearses rather than commits, and irreversible
operations never leave the human gate. That is mitigation, not immunity.

### 2. Text-to-text harm

The gate is on *actions*. An injection that makes the agent write a misleading
report, or address instructions to the operator, passes untouched. Explicit non-goal
for CaMeL, Progent, FIDES and f-secure alike, and the channel grows as people act on
more agent-written text.

### 3. Side channels and implicit flows

CaMeL demonstrates three against itself — an indirect-dependency leak, an
exception-triggered one-bit leak, a timing channel — and says "other types of side
channels might be possible." **EchoLeak (CVE-2025-32711, CVSS 9.3)** exfiltrated from
Microsoft 365 Copilot *through a Teams proxy that the content security policy allowed*.
The deterministic control was present and correctly enforced; the data left through the
hole it permitted.

### 4. Provenance

Every labelling scheme rests on an oracle deciding what is trusted, and ours assumes
the operator's instruction is. If an operator pastes attacker-controlled text, the
labels are wrong at the source and everything downstream inherits it. This is
under-specified in **every** system surveyed, ours included.

## What our evidence is, precisely

| | |
|---|---|
| Attacker knowledge | **Full defence specification** — operation table, snapshot contents, all five gates. Kerckhoffs, not obscurity. |
| Attacker type | Two frontier LLMs (Gemini 3.6 Flash, Claude), prompted to break it |
| Attack family | **Black-box, generation-only.** No gradient optimisation, no GCG, no query-feedback search |
| Budget | One generation per case; **no iterative refinement against observed failures** |
| n | 1,000 (500 per family) |
| Outcome measured | **End-state** — did an actuator run — not gate activation |
| Utility | Reported, 14/14 |

**The honest weakness:** our attacker never gets to see a failure and try again.
Nasr et al.'s search attack found its first success in a median of 13–93 queries; human
red-teamers broke every scenario they attempted. A one-shot generative attacker is a
real attacker but a weak one, and 1,000 of them is not the same as one persistent one.

## The unclaimed ground this sits in

There is a real gap in the literature, stated by the only paper that squarely addresses
deterministic action-side monitors
([arXiv:2606.26479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26479)):

> "every one of them is validated only on static benchmarks… the same methodology that
> made in-band defenses look strong until adaptive, defense-aware attacks broke twelve
> of them at over 90% success… **Either outcome is worth knowing, and neither is known
> today.**"

Notably, Nasr et al. **excluded** this defence family from their study because "the
attack is guaranteed to fail in most of the selected scenarios as the planning phase is
done independently of the triggers, i.e., control flow is data-independent."

Read that carefully in both directions. It is the strongest available evidence that a
deterministic gate is a categorically different target from a classifier — **and** it
means the family is *unmeasured*, not proven. Our 1,000 cases are a contribution to an
empty column, not a victory over a contested one.

## What would falsify this

- A single case reaching an actuator (the CI test fails)
- An adaptive attacker with query feedback — GCG, or a search loop that learns from
  refusals
- Any composition of permitted operations that produces harm the gates were meant to
  prevent
- A provenance-spoofing attack that makes attacker text read as operator instruction

The third is the one we expect to lose, and we would rather say so first.
